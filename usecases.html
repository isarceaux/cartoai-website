Use Cases

Use Case 1: Residential Energy Efficiency Assessment

Description: A homeowner wants to understand where their house is losing heat to improve energy efficiency and reduce heating bills.

Steps:

  1. User uploads thermal images of their house captured during cold weather.
  2. The system analyzes the thermal images to identify areas of significant heat loss (e.g., around windows, doors, insulation gaps).
  3. The system generates a report highlighting problematic areas and suggesting potential improvements (e.g., sealing gaps, adding insulation, upgrading windows).

Expected Outcome: The homeowner receives a clear and actionable report to help them prioritize energy efficiency upgrades.

Use Case 2: Building Inspection and Maintenance

Description: A building inspector needs to quickly assess the condition of a building's exterior for potential issues like water leaks, structural problems, or electrical faults that manifest as thermal anomalies.

Steps:

  1. The inspector uses a drone equipped with a thermal camera to capture images of the building facade.
  2. The images are processed by the system to detect abnormal temperature variations.
  3. The system generates a report or highlights areas on a building plan where potential issues are detected, along with the likely cause based on thermal patterns.

Expected Outcome: The inspector can quickly identify areas requiring closer examination, saving time and potentially detecting hidden problems.

Use Case 3: Urban Heat Island Analysis

Description: Urban planners and environmental scientists want to analyze the distribution of heat within a city to identify urban heat islands and assess the impact of different surfaces (e.g., green spaces, buildings, asphalt) on temperature.

Steps:

  1. Aerial thermal imagery of the urban area is collected (e.g., from aircraft or satellites).
  2. The system processes the imagery to create a thermal map of the city.
  3. The thermal map is overlaid with other geographic data (e.g., land use, vegetation cover) to analyze the correlation between surface types and temperature.

Expected Outcome: Insights are gained into the formation and intensity of urban heat islands, informing strategies for mitigation through urban design and planning.

Use Case 4: Infrastructure Monitoring

Description: Utility companies need to monitor critical infrastructure like power lines, pipelines, or bridges for signs of overheating, leaks, or structural stress that can be detected using thermal imaging.

Steps:

  1. Thermal data is collected from the infrastructure (e.g., using handheld cameras, drones, or mounted sensors).
  2. The system analyzes the thermal data to identify hot spots, temperature gradients, or other anomalies.
  3. Alerts or reports are generated for maintenance crews to investigate and address potential issues before they lead to failures.

Expected Outcome: Early detection of potential problems reduces the risk of service disruptions, enhances safety, and lowers maintenance costs.

Use Case 5: Environmental Monitoring and Wildlife Detection

Description: Conservationists and researchers use thermal imaging to monitor wildlife, track animal movements, or assess the health of ecosystems by detecting thermal signatures.

Steps:

  1. Thermal imagery is collected in the target environment (e.g., from drones, ground-based cameras, or aerial surveys).
  2. The system uses object detection and tracking algorithms to identify and monitor animals based on their thermal signatures.
  3. Data on animal presence, location, and behavior is recorded and analyzed for ecological studies or conservation efforts.

Expected Outcome: Improved ability to monitor wildlife populations, understand their distribution and behavior, and assess the impact of environmental changes.
